## Detailed Explanation

A professional videographer brings experience, multiple cameras and editing. If that isn't in the budget, you can still get a usable recording of the ceremony with equipment you or your guests already own. The goal is modest and achievable: a stable picture of the whole ceremony with clearly audible vows.

### Camera setup

Two fixed cameras cover most ceremonies well:

- **A wide shot** from the back or side of the aisle, showing the couple, officiant and some of the guests. This is your safety shot that captures everything.
- **A closer shot** angled at the couple's faces, usually from the front row or the side, framed so it still works if people shift a little.

Phones work fine. Use tripods or stable clamps, record in landscape, and turn on airplane mode and do-not-disturb so a call doesn't interrupt the recording. Check storage space and battery, and bring power banks for a long ceremony.

### Audio is the hard part

The most common disappointment with DIY ceremony video is that the vows can't be heard. A phone at the back of the room picks up shuffling, wind and coughing more clearly than quiet voices at the front. Options that help:

- A small audio recorder or spare phone recording audio near the officiant.
- A clip-on microphone on the officiant or one partner, recording to a phone in a pocket.
- If the venue has a sound system, asking whether you can record directly from it.

Recording audio separately means it will need to be lined up with the video later, which is simple in most editing apps.

### Put one person in charge

Choose one calm, reliable guest who isn't in the wedding party. Give them a short checklist: start all devices before the processional, check they're still recording after the vows, stop them after the recessional, and collect the footage. Ask them not to touch the framing during the ceremony unless something has clearly gone wrong.

### Test it first

Do a test at the rehearsal or a site visit, ideally at the same time of day so the light is similar. Record a minute of someone speaking at the altar and play it back. This is when you'll find out the outdoor wind noise problem or the backlit couple, not on the day.

## Common Scenarios

- **Outdoor ceremony.** Wind is the main audio problem. Use a foam or fur windscreen on any mic and keep recorders close to the speakers.
- **Very small ceremony or elopement.** One tripod and a clip-on mic may be all you need.
- **You'd like a short highlight film too.** Ask guests to share their clips, then hire a freelance editor to combine them with your ceremony footage.
- **Your venue restricts equipment.** Ask the coordinator what's allowed and where tripods can stand before you plan anything else.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I just ask a guest to film on their phone?**  
You can, but a hand-held phone tends to be shaky, interrupted by notifications and poor for audio. A tripod and a separate audio source give much better results with almost no extra effort.

**Should I livestream as well?**  
If some guests can't attend, a livestream is useful, but it's a separate job. Ideally give it its own device and person so it doesn't interfere with the recording.

**Do I need to tell the photographer?**  
Yes. Let them know where cameras will be so they can avoid standing in front of them and keep them out of their own shots where possible.

**Who should edit the footage?**  
Simple trimming and syncing can be done in free apps. For something more polished, a freelance video editor can work from your files after the wedding.
