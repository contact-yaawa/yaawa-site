## Detailed Explanation

Café weddings suit couples who want something small, informal and personal, often in a place that already means something to them. They also tend to keep costs down because the space, furniture and kitchen already exist. The trade-offs are capacity, flexibility and how used the staff are to events.

### Start with capacity and layout

Ask the café how many people it can seat for a meal, not how many it can hold standing. Then think about what else you need space for: a ceremony area if you're marrying there, a spot for a cake, room for a speech, and possibly a small dance floor. Sketch the room or ask for a floor plan. Some cafés can move tables around; others have fixed seating.

### Understand the hire terms

Cafés usually offer private hire in one of a few ways: a flat fee to close for your event, a minimum spend on food and drink, or a combination. Ask:

- What time can you arrive to set up, and when must everyone leave?
- Is there a minimum spend, and what counts towards it?
- Are service charges or gratuities added?
- Can you bring in your own cake, flowers, alcohol or decorations, and are there fees for doing so?
- Does the café have the licenses needed to serve alcohol or play amplified music?

Get the answers in writing. Small businesses can be flexible, but that flexibility is only useful if both sides remember the same agreement.

### Plan food and drink with the café

Ask what they can realistically serve for your numbers. Many cafés do well with brunch, afternoon tea, sharing plates or a set menu. Ask whether they can handle dietary requirements and how food will be served, whether at the table, from a counter or as a buffet.

### Think about the ceremony

If the café can't host the ceremony, many couples marry at a registry office, park or nearby venue and walk or drive to the café for the meal. If you do marry at the café, check where guests will stand or sit and whether the space works acoustically for vows.

### Keep décor simple

A café's look is part of the appeal. A few flowers, candles where permitted, a sign and some personal touches are usually enough. Check what you're allowed to attach to walls or tables.

## Common Scenarios

- **The café closes in the early evening.** Plan a daytime wedding, or move to a bar or someone's home for an after-party.
- **You need more seats than the café holds.** Consider a ceremony elsewhere with a larger guest list and a smaller café meal afterwards for close family and friends.
- **The café has never hosted a wedding.** Walk the owner through the timeline and assign a friend or day-of coordinator to be the point of contact on the day.
- **You'd like music.** Ask whether background music, a small acoustic act or a playlist is allowed, and at what volume.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a café wedding cheaper than a traditional venue?**  
Often, because the space, furniture and kitchen are already there. Compare the hire fee or minimum spend with what you'd pay elsewhere for venue, catering and rentals combined.

**How many guests can a café wedding have?**  
It depends entirely on the café. Small cafés may suit only a handful of guests; larger ones with outdoor space can hold more.

**Do we need a planner for a café wedding?**  
Not necessarily, but having one person, whether a coordinator or a reliable friend, handle questions on the day lets you and the café staff focus on the event.

**Can we decorate the café?**  
Usually, within limits. Ask what can be moved, attached or lit, and when you can set up.
