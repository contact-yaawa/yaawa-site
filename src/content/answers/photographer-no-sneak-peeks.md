## Detailed Explanation

A sneak peek is a small set of edited photos that some photographers send within days of the wedding, before the full gallery is ready. Many couples now expect them because they're common on social media, but they're a goodwill gesture, not an industry rule. Whether a missing sneak peek is a problem depends on what you agreed to and how the photographer communicates.

### Start with the contract

Your contract is the reference point. Look for three things: whether sneak peeks or previews are mentioned at all, the stated delivery window for the full gallery, and how the photographer says they'll communicate after the wedding. If previews are promised, it's reasonable to ask where they are. If the contract only gives a delivery window for the full gallery and that window hasn't ended, the photographer is working within what you agreed.

### Why some photographers don't send them

Some photographers prefer to edit an entire wedding in one go so the color and style stay consistent across every image. Others have had previews misused, for example cropped, filtered or posted without credit before the gallery was finished. Some simply have a backlog during busy season and would rather put their time into finishing your full set. None of these is a red flag on its own.

### When it's worth following up

It makes sense to get in touch if the contract promised previews and none have arrived, if the delivery date has passed, or if you've sent a message and heard nothing back. Keep the first message short and neutral: thank them for the day, ask whether previews are part of their process, and ask for a rough timeline for the full gallery. Most delays are resolved by a single friendly email.

If you still get no answer after a reasonable wait, follow up again in writing and keep copies. Written communication protects both sides if a real problem develops.

### What to do while you wait

Ask guests to send you their phone photos and videos, or set up a shared album. It won't replace the professional gallery, but it gives you something to look at and share in the meantime.

## Common Scenarios

- **The contract promises sneak peeks and none have arrived.** Send a polite message that references the contract and asks when to expect them.
- **The contract doesn't mention sneak peeks.** Ask once whether a few previews are possible. If the answer is no, that's within their rights.
- **The full gallery is past its delivery date.** Contact the photographer in writing, ask for an updated date and keep a record of the exchange.
- **The photographer has stopped replying.** Follow up by email and any other channel listed in the contract. Silence after several attempts is worth taking more seriously than a missing preview.
- **Family keeps asking for photos.** Share guest photos and explain that the professional gallery is being edited.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are sneak peeks a standard part of wedding photography?**  
They're common but not universal. Treat them as something to confirm with your photographer rather than assume.

**Can I ask for sneak peeks if they're not in my contract?**  
Yes, a polite request is fine. Just accept that the photographer can say no.

**How should I word the request?**  
Thank them, ask whether previews are part of their process, and ask when the full gallery is likely to be ready. Keep it brief and friendly.

**What should I check before booking a photographer next time?**  
Ask about previews, the delivery timeline and how they communicate after the event, and make sure the answers are in the contract.
