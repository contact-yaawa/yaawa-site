## Detailed Explanation
### 1. What the wording usually means  
British wedding invitations have long followed a fairly standardized format. When the card says something like:

> **Reception**  
> The Rose Garden Restaurant  
> Friday 12 June, 19:00  

the default assumption is that the reception includes a sit‑down dinner (or at least a plated or buffet meal) unless the invitation adds a qualifier such as “cocktail reception only” or “drinks reception to follow.” The time of 19:00 is also a clue: a 7 p.m. start typically signals a full evening programme—welcome drinks, dinner, speeches, and dancing. A “drinks reception” would more often begin earlier (around 17:00–18:00) and be followed by a separate invitation to the wedding ceremony or a later dinner at a different location.

### 2. Why couples ask about dietary requirements  
From the host’s perspective, catering for a restaurant wedding can be a logistical puzzle. Restaurants often require a final headcount and a list of allergies at least a week in advance. If a guest fails to disclose a nut, gluten, or dairy intolerance, the kitchen may inadvertently serve a dish that could cause a serious reaction. Conversely, if a guest tells the couple they are vegetarian but the menu is already fixed, the venue may have to prepare a special plate, which can increase costs.

By asking early, you give the couple the chance to:

- **Communicate with the chef** – Many upscale venues have a dedicated liaison who can flag allergens or create separate plates.  
- **Budget accurately** – Special diets sometimes require extra ingredients or staff time. Knowing this ahead of time prevents surprise charges.  
- **Maintain a smooth service flow** – The kitchen can label special dishes, preventing mix‑ups during a busy reception.  

### 3. The etiquette of “assuming” versus “asking”  
Assuming you’re invited to the dinner is not impolite; it’s the norm. The etiquette rule is simply: *If you’re not sure, ask.* The key is how you phrase the question. A polite, “We’re excited to join you! Could you let us know if the reception includes dinner? I also have a shellfish allergy, so I’d love to know the menu options,” accomplishes two things:

1. **Confirms the invitation’s intent** – It lets the couple clarify if the event is a drinks‑only gathering.  
2. **Provides essential health information** – It signals that you respect their planning and want to help avoid any mishaps.

Most couples will appreciate the clarification because it saves them from a last‑minute scramble. Even if the invitation was clearly a dinner, a brief note about allergies is seen as courteous rather than presumptuous.

### 4. Potential pitfalls of the “wrong assumption”  
If the invitation truly meant a drinks reception only (a less common but still possible scenario), your question could unintentionally highlight a mistake in the couple’s wording. In that case, the hosts may need to either:

- **Update the invitation** – Send a quick email or text to clarify that dinner is not included, perhaps offering a recommendation for a nearby eatery.  
- **Offer a compromise** – Some couples might arrange a light snack or a small platter for guests with dietary restrictions, even if the main plan was drinks only.  

These adjustments can create a bit of stress for the couple, especially if they are on a tight budget. That’s why it’s helpful to read the invitation carefully first; look for phrases such as “cocktail reception only” or “reception followed by dinner at a later venue.” If the wording is ambiguous, a gentle query is still better than assuming incorrectly.

### 5. Cultural nuance in the UK wedding scene  
British wedding etiquette traditionally leans toward formality, but modern couples are increasingly informal, especially in city venues like restaurants. Even in a relaxed setting, the core etiquette principles remain the same:

- **RSVP promptly** – Respond within the time‑frame indicated (usually by the end of May for a June wedding).  
- **Mention dietary needs with your RSVP** – Most invitation cards have a line for “Allergies / Dietary Requirements.”  
- **Avoid making last‑minute changes** – Once the headcount is set, the restaurant may not be able to accommodate new requests.  

If you missed the dietary line on the card, a short follow‑up email or text is perfectly acceptable. The couple will likely be grateful for the reminder.

### 6. How to phrase your response for maximum clarity  
Here are three sample wordings that strike the right balance between gratitude, clarification, and health‑safety:

1. **Standard friendly tone**  
   > “Thank you so much for inviting us to celebrate your special day! We’re looking forward to joining you at The Lantern at 19:00. Could you confirm whether the reception includes dinner? I have a dairy intolerance, so I’d love to know the menu options in advance.”

2. **Brief and to the point**  
   > “We’re delighted to attend your reception on 12 June. Just to confirm, is dinner part of the programme? I’m allergic to peanuts, so please let the chef know.”

3. **If you suspect a drinks‑only event**  
   > “Thank you for the invitation! We’ll be there at 19:00. Is the evening planned as a drinks reception only, or is there a dinner served as well? I have a gluten sensitivity and would appreciate any details.”

Each version communicates respect for the couple’s plans, shows you’re proactive about health concerns, and avoids any impression of entitlement.

### 7. When the couple replies “yes, dinner is included”  
If the answer is a clear “yes,” you can simply thank them and restate your dietary restriction:

> “That’s wonderful—thanks for confirming! I’ll be sure to let the restaurant know about my shellfish allergy. Can’t wait to celebrate with you both.”

If the couple says “no, it’s drinks only,” you have a few options:

- **Accept graciously** – Thank them for the clarification and enjoy the cocktails and conversation.  
- **Plan a pre‑ or post‑reception meal** – Arrange to meet a friend for dinner before the reception or order a takeaway for after the event.  
- **Offer to bring a suitable snack** – If you’re comfortable, you could discreetly bring a small, allergy‑safe bite for yourself (e.g., a gluten‑free bar), but always check the venue’s policy first.

### 8. Managing expectations for other guests  
Remember that you are not the only guest with dietary considerations. When you ask about the menu, you also help the couple gauge how many special meals they need to request. If you notice several guests with similar restrictions, it might be worth suggesting a “flexi‑menu” (e.g., a vegetarian main with optional meat add‑ons) to the couple, but only if they ask for your opinion. The couple may already have a plan in place, especially if they used a professional wedding planner or a venue that handles catering.

### 9. Legal and safety aspects  
In the UK, the Food Standards Agency mandates that restaurants must provide allergen information on request. While the venue is legally obliged to accommodate known allergies, the onus is still on the guest to disclose them. By informing the couple early, you are participating in a safety net that protects both yourself and the venue from potential liability.

### 10. Bottom line for the modern couple  
- **Read the invitation carefully** – Look for clues about the type of reception.  
- **Respond promptly with your RSVP** – Include any allergies in the designated line.  
- **If uncertain, ask politely** – A short, friendly query is always welcome.  
- **Follow up if the menu changes** – Restaurants sometimes adjust dishes a few days before the event; a quick check‑in can confirm that your dietary needs remain covered.  

By following these steps, you contribute to a seamless, enjoyable celebration for everyone involved.

## Common Scenarios
- **Scenario 1 – The invitation says “Reception at 19:00, drinks to follow.”**  
  *Interpretation:* Likely drinks‑only. Send a brief reply confirming you understand it’s a cocktail hour and mention any allergies in case the venue offers small bites.  

- **Scenario 2 – The card includes a line for “Dietary Requirements” but you missed it.**  
  *Action:* Email or text the couple, referencing the RSVP and adding, “I’m allergic to soy – could you let the venue know?”  

- **Scenario 3 – You have multiple allergies (e.g., nuts and gluten).**  
  *Tip:* List them clearly in one sentence and ask if the chef can prepare a safe plate. Offer to share a copy of your allergy card if needed.  

- **Scenario 4 – The couple replies “yes, dinner is included” but you didn’t receive a menu.**  
  *Solution:* Politely ask, “Could you please share the menu or let me know the main options? I want to ensure my shellfish allergy is covered.”  

- **Scenario 5 – The reception is at a trendy tapas bar with a “share‑plates” format.**  
  *Consideration:* Ask whether the dishes are prepared with shared sauces that might contain allergens, and request a few plated options if needed.  

- **Scenario 6 – You’re the plus‑one of a guest and the invitation doesn’t list your name.**  
  *Best practice:* Confirm with the primary guest that you’re welcome, then follow the same etiquette steps regarding dinner assumptions and dietary needs.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**What if the invitation doesn’t mention food at all?**  
Even if the wording is vague, the default in the UK is that a reception includes a meal unless stated otherwise. It’s perfectly acceptable to reply with a short, polite query about the dinner and to note any allergies. Most couples will respond quickly to clarify.

**Can I bring my own food if I have a severe allergy?**  
If you have a life‑threatening allergy, it’s reasonable to discuss this with the couple or the venue ahead of time. Some restaurants allow guests to bring a safe alternative, especially if the kitchen cannot guarantee a completely allergen‑free environment. Always ask the couple first; they may be able to arrange a special plate for you.

**Is it rude to ask the venue directly about the menu?**  
Ideally, you should go through the couple. They are the ones coordinating the catering and may have negotiated a set menu with the restaurant. Directly contacting the venue could bypass the couple’s planning and cause confusion. If you haven’t heard back after a reasonable time, a gentle follow‑up to the couple is the best route.

**What if the couple says dinner isn’t included and I’m left hungry?**  
Treat it as an opportunity to explore the local food scene! Many restaurants have a bar menu or small plates that guests can order during the drinks reception. If you’re concerned, you could eat a light snack beforehand or arrange to meet a friend for a pre‑reception bite.

**How far in advance should I disclose my dietary restrictions?**  
Aim to include them with your RSVP, which is usually due 3–4 weeks before the wedding. If you realize a new allergy after sending your RSVP, contact the couple as soon as possible—ideally at least one week before the event—to give the venue enough time to adjust.

**Will the couple be upset if I ask about food?**  
Most couples view such questions as helpful. They are often juggling many details, and a clear, courteous inquiry prevents surprises on the day. As long as you ask politely and thank them for their effort, you’re unlikely to cause any offense.
