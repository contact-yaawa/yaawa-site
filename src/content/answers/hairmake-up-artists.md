## Detailed Explanation

Hair and makeup is one of the few wedding services you experience up close for the whole day, and every photo shows it. Choosing an artist is mostly about three things: whether they're good at the look you want on someone like you, whether they can work within your schedule and budget, and whether the arrangements are clear in writing.

### Read portfolios for your situation, not just for beauty

Almost every artist's portfolio looks good. What matters is whether it shows people with your skin tone, skin type and hair texture, and looks close to what you want. Someone who mostly does soft natural makeup may not be the right person for a dramatic look, and the reverse is also true. If you can't find relevant examples, ask the artist directly whether they have them.

### Book a trial

A trial is your chance to test the look, see how the artist works and check how it holds up. Bring reference photos, wear a top with a neckline similar to your dress, and if possible schedule it on a day you're going out afterwards so you can see how it lasts. Take photos in daylight and with flash. Afterwards, ask yourself whether the artist listened, adjusted when you asked, and explained what they were doing.

### Work out the timing

Each person takes time, and the artist needs to finish everyone before photos start. Give the artist your headcount and photo start time and ask them to build a schedule. If the numbers don't fit, they may bring an assistant, or some people may need to start very early. It's better to know this now than on the morning.

### Get the details in writing

The contract should list the price per person, trial fee, travel or early-start charges, how many people are included, start and finish times, the deposit and cancellation terms, and what happens if the artist is ill or can't come. A professional will have answers to all of these.

### Ways to save money

If the budget is tight, you don't have to book a professional for everyone. Common options are booking the artist for the person getting married only, having the wedding party do their own makeup while the artist does hair (or the other way round), or asking for a reduced rate on simpler looks. Ask what the artist offers; many have different packages.

## Common Scenarios

- **You loved the trial but the artist's schedule is tight.** Ask whether they can bring an assistant or start earlier, and get the revised timing in the contract.
- **You didn't like the trial.** Tell the artist specifically what didn't work. If a second attempt still isn't right, it's fine to book someone else.
- **Your wedding party wants the artist too, but the budget doesn't stretch.** Book the artist for yourself and suggest the wedding party pay for their own services if they want them.
- **You can't find anyone experienced with your hair texture.** Ask local salons who specialise in your hair type for recommendations, and ask any artist you consider for examples of similar work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When should I book a hair and makeup artist?**  
As early as practical once you have a date and venue, especially for popular dates and seasons.

**Is a trial really necessary?**  
It isn't required, but it's the best way to avoid surprises. Many problems are easy to fix at a trial and hard to fix on the morning.

**Should the artist come to me or should I go to a salon?**  
Having the artist come to you saves travel on the day but often adds a fee. Compare the cost with the time and stress of everyone travelling to a salon.

**What should I tip?**  
Tipping customs vary by country and by whether the artist owns the business. Check the contract and ask what is customary where you live.
